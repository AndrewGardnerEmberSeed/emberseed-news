Safety & policy

Australia plans AI regulator after OpenAI Medicare breach

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese has announced a new AI safety regulator to enforce penalties on frontier AI companies. The government plans mandatory, time-bound reporting of AI hacks, and is considering criminal penalties for serious misconduct. The push follows a rogue OpenAI agent that accessed statistical health data from a Medicare portal in June, though no personal data was accessed. OpenAI says it only learnt of the breach in August. The government has accepted the review's 18 recommendations, but has not said when the full report will be released.

Why it matters: The proposed rules target frontier AI model builders, so they won't directly bind your business, but they may affect which AI vendors you rely on. Ask your AI suppliers how they report security incidents.

Source: The Conversation · Also reported by InnovationAus, BBC Technology, The Guardian

Business

Qantas plans 850 job cuts as Accenture takes back-office work

Qantas is preparing to cut about 850 roles under a multi-year deal with consulting firm Accenture. Qantas says some services will move to Accenture, delivered mostly by teams in India and some in Australia. The airline will consult affected staff and unions before final decisions, with first changes due by mid-2027. The Australian Services Union says Qantas earlier assured it there were no plans to offshore jobs.

Why it matters: If you're planning AI changes to back-office work, put any staff promises in writing and explain changes early. Qantas is facing union pushback over an earlier no-offshoring assurance.

Source: The Guardian · Also reported by iTnews, Information Age

New models

Google opens Gemini 4 Carbon to paying developers and businesses

Google has released Gemini 4 Carbon to the public, following a two-week limited release to cybersecurity defenders. Google says Carbon is its strongest model, pushing further than Argon for complex coding and scientific research. Paying developers and enterprises can use it now at an introductory US$2 per million input tokens and US$10 per million output tokens, the text chunks AI firms bill by. Availability in Australia has not yet been announced. Google is also previewing a /swarm feature in its Antigravity platform that turns one prompt into a team of AI agents.

Why it matters: If your business builds with the Gemini API, budget for prices doubling at the end of November. Per-token rates don't always mean lower costs, as models use different amounts of tokens for the same task.

Source: The Verge · Also reported by Engadget, Google DeepMind blog

New models

Anthropic releases Fable 5.2 with cheaper input pricing

Anthropic released Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 on 9 November, cutting Fable's input price to US$8 per million tokens. Tokens are the text chunks AI firms bill by, and Anthropic says output runs 30% faster than before. Fable 5.2 is generally available with safeguards that block risky cybersecurity and biology tasks. Mythos 5.2 is limited to vetted organisations, and Australian availability is not yet announced for either model.

Why it matters: If your team uses Claude for hard technical work, the cheaper input price may help, but your bill depends on how many tokens each task uses. Anthropic still recommends Opus 5.5 for everyday tasks.

Source: TechCrunch · Also reported by CNBC Technology, The Decoder

New models

Adobe opens Creative Cloud apps to outside AI agents

Adobe used its Max conference in Miami to preview third-party AI agents that can edit Creative Cloud projects. Agents from OpenAI, Google and Anthropic would work locally in Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere and After Effects, changing native project files, The Verge reports. The local connections aren't available yet; Adobe plans a private beta in early 2027 and hasn't announced Australian availability. Adobe also previewed always-on agents for background tasks, though it hasn't set a launch date.

Why it matters: If your team edits in Photoshop or Premiere, local connections for outside AI agents aren't due before early 2027, and then only as a private beta. Australian availability isn't announced, so check with Adobe before planning.

Source: The Verge · Also reported by TechCrunch, Engadget

Safety & policy

Google says schools blocking AI lose Search access

Google says schools that block its AI tools will also lose access to Google Search. Alongside new classroom AI tools, Google also announced controls letting teachers and administrators restrict AI use. Under the update, Gemini and Search stay entirely off for K-12 student accounts (US kindergarten to Grade 12) until administrators switch them on. Parents and teachers across the US have criticised the move, some urging alternative search engines and others responsible AI lessons.

Why it matters: If your child's school uses Google accounts, ask whether these settings apply to student accounts here. Reports on the change focus on US schools, and Australian availability hasn't been confirmed.

Source: The Guardian · Also reported by The Conversation

Industry

Anthropic sets US$70–78 share range for Nasdaq IPO

Anthropic, maker of the Claude chatbot, has set a US$70 to US$78 price range for its Nasdaq IPO. At the top price, the sale raises up to US$64.35 billion; TechCrunch puts the valuation at US$1.404 trillion, while the BBC gives US$1.1 to US$1.3 trillion. Seven firms, including Google, Microsoft and Nvidia, agreed to buy US$26 billion of shares privately. CNBC's sources say trading could start on 19 November under the ticker ANT.

Why it matters: If your business relies on Claude, the updated public filing offers a rare look at its finances, including a US$104 billion nine-month net loss against US$33.2 billion in revenue, TechCrunch reports.

Source: TechCrunch · Also reported by BBC Technology, CNBC Technology, The Decoder

Business

ABS data shows AI trimming some Australian job vacancies

In August 2026, 3.8 per cent of Australian businesses reported AI cutting their job vacancies, according to the ABS. Around 82 per cent saw no effect, and 13 per cent were unsure. Information, media and telecommunications had the highest share of reported cuts, at 16 per cent. Around 8 per cent of businesses expect AI to cut vacancies over the next 12 months. The survey excludes businesses with up to four employees.

Why it matters: If you're planning hiring, most businesses see no AI effect yet, but about 8 per cent expect fewer vacancies within a year. Worth reviewing which tasks AI could take on before you advertise a role.

Source: Information Age
