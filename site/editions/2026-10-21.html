Safety & policy

Meta pauses Muse chat sharing after public links found

Meta has paused the sharing feature in its Muse AI assistant after security firm Claroc found shared chats publicly searchable. Claroc says it found more than 6,800 share links through public DNS records, which it describes as a directory for web traffic. Some exposed chats included a marketing company's revenue review, a loan application and a workplace's staff calendar. Meta says it is reviewing the report and considering changes to how the links can be discovered.

Why it matters: If your team uses an AI assistant's share links, treat them as public until your provider confirms a fix. Keep client finances and staff details out of shared chats.

Source: The Verge · Also reported by Ars Technica, Fast Company

Safety & policy

US orders federal agencies to drop DeepSeek AI tools

On 19 October, the US Department of Homeland Security ordered federal civilian agencies to stop buying DeepSeek products and remove them from government IT systems. The order also covers third-party tools built on DeepSeek technology, though agencies can request exceptions. DHS warns that sensitive data from US government users could be accessible to Chinese authorities under Chinese law. China's Foreign Ministry opposes overstretching national security concerns and says it will defend Chinese firms' interests.

Why it matters: If you sell to US government agencies, check whether your AI tools or suppliers use DeepSeek, since the order covers products built on it and bars DeepSeek from federal contracts. It applies to US civilian agencies, not Australian buyers.

Source: TechCrunch · Also reported by Engadget, The Guardian, BBC Technology

Applications

Yelp's AI agent will phone restaurants for wait times

Yelp is adding Check for Me, a feature that lets app users have an AI voice agent call a restaurant to check wait times. Users enter their party size and phone number on a restaurant page, and the agent texts back the results. A new OpenTable integration in Yelp's AI assistant lets users book tables without leaving the app. TechCrunch notes that when two agents talk to each other, the conversation might break down.

Why it matters: If your venue takes phone bookings, Yelp's agent may start calling to ask about wait times, as other AI assistants already call businesses. Yelp has not yet announced Australian availability for Check for Me.

Source: TechCrunch

Gaming & entertainment

Spotify removes 50 million AI spam tracks from recommendations

Spotify says it has removed more than 50 million songs it considers AI-generated spam or low-quality "slop" from user recommendations over the past year. The streaming service made the announcement on Tuesday, adding that its recommendation system now highlights more music from human artists. It has also introduced an "AI Persona" badge for artists who use an AI identity and don't represent a real person, and it won't recommend their music. Spotify has stopped short of requiring labels on all AI tracks, and says listener complaints about such content have dropped.

Why it matters: If you upload music to Spotify, expect tracks that look like AI spam to be filtered out of recommendations. Spotify uses listener feedback, upload patterns and detection tools to flag them.

Source: The Verge

Applications

Adobe brings one-tap eye opening to Lightroom mobile

Adobe has added an Open Eyes tool to Lightroom's mobile app on iOS, iPadOS and Android. When the app detects closed eyes, it shows a one-tap icon that uses generative AI to open them. It draws on other photos of the same person in your library so the eyes match their own colour, size and shape. Free users get two tries before a subscription prompt, according to DPReview, and Australian availability is not yet announced.

Why it matters: If you edit customer or event photos on your phone, this could save reshoots, but free Lightroom users only get two tries before a subscription prompt.

Source: The Verge

New models

Mistral launches Voxtral 3 voice agent models for real-time conversations

Mistral AI launched Voxtral 3 on 20 October, with transcription priced from $0.0035 per minute, according to The Decoder, which doesn't name the currency. The package combines speech-to-text, text-to-speech and a LiveKit-based voice agent SDK, a toolkit for building agents, in one streaming pipeline. Transcription covers more than 50 languages, and speech output covers more than 35 languages and 50 dialects. Mistral says it can run on your own infrastructure or through its EU-hosted API, and agents can book appointments or check invoices mid-call.

Why it matters: If you take phone or web bookings, a voice agent could handle simple requests. Mistral's hosting is in the EU or on your own systems, and Australian availability and pricing aren't announced, so check where call data is processed.

Source: The Decoder · Also reported by Mistral AI news

Industry

Salesforce agrees to buy Braze for US$5.66 billion

Salesforce has agreed to buy customer engagement platform Braze for about US$5.66 billion, according to CNBC. Braze makes AI marketing tools that manage customer data and messaging. Braze shareholders will receive US$47.50 in cash per share. Braze shares jumped more than 50% on Tuesday after closing at US$30.04 the day before. Salesforce says the deal should close in fiscal 2028, subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals.

Why it matters: If your business uses Braze for customer messaging, a change of owner is planned, but the deal isn't expected to close until fiscal 2028 and still needs shareholder and regulatory approvals. Braze's CEO says it will keep building as Braze.

Source: CNBC Technology

Industry

Otter.ai lands US$320 million from General Catalyst fund

Otter.ai has raised US$320 million from General Catalyst's Customer Value Fund, it announced on Tuesday. The fund provides capital without diluting equity and earns returns based on the results of marketing spend. Otter says it will use the money for enterprise growth, go-to-market strategy, international expansion and product innovation. It says 40 million people have used its product to date, up from 25 million last year, but gave no monthly active user or churn figures.

Why it matters: If your team relies on AI meeting notes, the market is crowded, with many well-funded rivals. Compare features and costs before committing, and ask vendors how many customers keep using their product.

Source: TechCrunch
