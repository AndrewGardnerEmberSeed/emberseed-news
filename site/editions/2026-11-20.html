Industry

Anthropic prices record IPO at US$83 a share

Anthropic has priced its IPO at US$83 a share, above its planned range of US$70 to US$78. The Claude maker sold 980 million shares for US$81.3 billion, which TechCrunch says beats SpaceX's US$75 billion as the largest IPO by proceeds. At that price the company is valued at roughly US$1.5 trillion, below the US$2 trillion it reportedly sought. Shares are set to trade on Nasdaq as ANT from Thursday, 19 November.

Why it matters: If your team uses Claude, nothing changes in your plans today. Anthropic is now a listed company, so check its pricing and terms when your contracts come up for renewal.

Source: TechCrunch · Also reported by The Verge, Wired, The Guardian

New models

Google launches Gemini 4 Neon, sitting between Carbon and Hydrogen

Google DeepMind has released Gemini 4 Neon, which it positions between its Carbon and Hydrogen models. Google says Neon ranks third overall on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, scoring 61 at its highest reasoning setting. Through the Gemini API, US$3 buys one million input tokens (the chunks of text AI firms bill by) and US$15 buys one million output tokens. Pro and Ultra subscribers can use Neon in the Gemini app, and Australian availability is not yet announced.

Why it matters: If your team uses Gemini through the API, compare its US$3 and US$15 per-million-token prices with your current model before switching. Pro and Ultra subscribers get Neon in the Gemini app.

Source: Google DeepMind blog · Also reported by The Decoder

Safety & policy

Easy Solutions' AI caller probes Australians' super funds

An AI caller named "Alex" pitched super reviews and deflected an ABC reporter's questions. Claiming to work for Easy Solutions Group, it repeatedly asked whether the caller's fund had ever offered a review. An ASIC review of 15 advice licensees found lead generators funnelled 7,639 clients into deals worth more than $678 million between early 2025 and mid-2026. Neither the bot nor its firm broke the law, and Easy Solutions did not reply to the ABC.

Why it matters: If your staff take cold calls about super or finances, assume the caller may be a bot, and don't share personal financial details. Ask who they work for first.

Source: ABC News

Safety & policy

Music industry rejects reported AI scraping carve-out for musicians

Australia's music industry is rejecting a reported plan to exempt musicians from AI scraping. The Australian Financial Review reported the government may let AI firms buy permits to train on Australian content, and music may be exempted. Aria CEO Annabelle Herd calls the idea unworkable, while Writers' Guild CEO Claire Pullen expects creatives to stay united. Guardian Australia could not verify the details, and the government would not comment.

Why it matters: If your business relies on original writing, images or music, the rules on AI training are still unsettled. The reported permit scheme is unconfirmed, so watch for a formal government position before changing your licensing or contracts.

Source: The Guardian

Applications

Canva adds a switch to turn off generative AI

Canva now lets users switch off its generative AI features, including the Magic Expand photo tool and image generation in its chatbot. The toggle doesn't cover everything: the background remover and Magic Grab stay on, because Canva says they don't generate anything new. A separate setting shows human-made content first in Canva's library, which Canva says contains only a small share of AI-generated elements. Both options work for free and paid users.

Why it matters: If your team designs in Canva, you can now switch off its generative AI tools under Profile > Settings > Permission. Background removal and Magic Grab stay on, so don't assume a fully AI-free setup.

Source: The Verge

Safety & policy

Citibank makes customers liable for AI agent activity

US bank Citibank has told customers that anything going wrong with a third-party AI finance tool is their problem, even if hackers get in. Its updated Online User Agreement says customers who grant account access to AI agents are responsible for activity through them. JPMorgan and Bank of America announced similar policies last month, and PNC changed its terms this week. Citizens has also emailed customers warning they are liable for what their AI assistant does, according to an email seen by TechCrunch.

Why it matters: If you give an AI tool your banking login, read your bank's terms first. Some US banks now make customers liable for losses, even after a hack. Check whether your bank has similar wording.

Source: TechCrunch

Gaming & entertainment

Gallium Studios' MyMems turns memories into AI game worlds

Will Wright, creator of SimCity and The Sims, is developing MyMems, a mobile social simulation built from players' own memories. Gallium Studios and partner Artificial Agency announced the game on 18 November, and it is set to launch in 2027. Players add memories by speaking or typing, and generative AI turns them into map pieces and avatars of people they know. No Australian release details have been announced.

Why it matters: If your business handles personal information with AI tools, ask vendors where it's stored and who controls it. The game's AI partner says its memory tool learns locally and only with permission.

Source: Ars Technica · Also reported by The Verge

Trends

IEA sees data centre emissions up 70% by 2030

The International Energy Agency says emissions from data centres are on track to rise 70% by 2030. Electricity use by the sector could double over the same period, with AI driving about three-quarters of the growth. The agency expects gas to supply about 60% of US data centre power by 2030, and coal about half of China's. CNBC reports the agency also warns that grid limits, local opposition and tight supply chains could delay new projects.

Why it matters: If you depend on cloud or AI services, expect possible delays to new capacity. Ask your providers where their servers are powered from.

Source: The Guardian · Also reported by CNBC Technology
