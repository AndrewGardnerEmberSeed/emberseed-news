Trends

US voters back data centre curbs in nearly every vote

Across 10 US states on 3 November, voters sided against data centres in all but one of 41 local ballot measures, according to Ballotpedia. The exception was Harwood, North Dakota, where voters rejected an extra review of large industrial projects. Ohio alone had 21 of the measures, and Democrat Sherrod Brown ousted Republican Senator Jon Husted in the Senate race. Engadget counted 45 measures, so tallies differ by tracker.

Why it matters: These were US local votes, so nothing changes in Australia yet. If your team relies on cloud or AI services, watch whether US data centre projects slow, as CNBC suggests they may.

Source: CNBC Technology · Also reported by Wired, Engadget, The Verge

Safety & policy

Meta quietly offers Australians an AI training opt-out

Meta has quietly offered Australians a way to stop their user data training its AI. The objection form has been live since July 2025, but Meta never announced it. Digital Rights Watch's Tom Sulston says people must dig through privacy settings to find it. The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner says it will publish the results of its investigation in coming months.

Why it matters: If you post publicly on Facebook or Instagram, your posts may be used to train Meta's AI unless you submit the objection form. Private accounts are already exempt.

Source: ABC News

New models

ChatGPT adds animated GIF creation with Images 2.6

OpenAI says ChatGPT can now create animated GIFs from a text prompt or an uploaded photo. The feature arrives with its new Images 2.6 model, rolling out to all ChatGPT and Codex users on web, desktop and mobile. Users can change a GIF's animation speed, edit individual frames and share the result. OpenAI also says users now generate over 4 billion images a week across ChatGPT and its API, up from 3 billion in September.

Why it matters: If your team makes social posts or simple product demos, ChatGPT can now produce a basic animated GIF from one prompt or photo. Timing for Australia is not yet announced, so check your app rather than assume.

Source: The Verge · Also reported by The Decoder

Safety & policy

OpenAI pledges to cover data centre power costs

On 5 November, OpenAI published a National Community Compact and a Compute Campus Policy Blueprint for its data centres. OpenAI says it will pay the full electricity cost of its sites, including upgrades power providers need. It also says it will publish water and power use per site and avoid non-disclosure deals with public officials. CNBC notes that all but one of 41 US local measures backed restrictions or rejected pro-development ones.

Why it matters: Not yet announced for Australia. If your business relies on AI services, these pledges preview what customers may ask providers about, such as power, water and local jobs.

Source: OpenAI News · Also reported by Engadget, CNBC Technology, The Decoder

Business

Uber says AI agents arranged just four rides

Uber executives told analysts on the third-quarter earnings call that third-party AI agents arranged only four rides, plus around 500 Uber Eats orders. Against roughly 320 million rides and deliveries, TechCrunch reports the figures cover the week of Halloween. CEO Dara Khosrowshahi called the traction "very, very early" and said Uber will work with third-party AI firms. He also said the app takes about 50 seconds to book a ride.

Why it matters: If customers order or book through a platform, AI agents aren't sending much business yet, so your own app or website still matters most. Uber says it will work with third-party agents, so the channel may grow.

Source: The Verge · Also reported by TechCrunch

Business

Apple trims daily AI spending caps for engineering teams

Apple is reportedly cutting the daily AI spending limits for its engineering teams, according to Bloomberg. The cap fell from US$300 to US$200 a day in October, with deeper cuts to US$100 for some allowances still planned. Outside engineering, Apple is weighing a limit as low as US$25 a day. The Decoder notes Microsoft, Tesla, Uber and Meta have also capped internal AI spending.

Why it matters: If your team pays for AI tools by usage, set a per-person daily or monthly limit now, before the bills arrive.

Source: TechCrunch · Also reported by The Decoder

Safety & policy

US midterms leave data centres politically contentious

An ABC News commentary argues that data centres became a major political issue at the US midterms. Of about 41 proposals put to voters in ten states, all but one are on track to lose. An NBC News exit poll found three-quarters of voters oppose data centres in their community. Recent Australian polling found 44 per cent of people would oppose one built near them, and the government's proposed data centre rules are expected by the end of the year.

Why it matters: If your business relies on cloud AI tools, watch the government's data centre rules, expected by year-end. Local opposition could slow where new capacity gets built.

Source: ABC News · Also reported by InnovationAus

Industry

Figma beats forecasts, but shares slip after results

Design software firm Figma beat Wall Street forecasts in its third quarter, with revenue up about 43% year on year, the company said. Its adjusted operating margin hit 12%, above the 6.1% analysts expected, and it raised its full-year revenue forecast. Shares fell about 7% in extended trading, and the company expects fourth-quarter growth to slow to about 33%. Figma also gave clients more AI credits while keeping prices unchanged.

Why it matters: If your team uses Figma, you may now get more AI credits at the same price, so check your plan before renewal.

Source: CNBC Technology
