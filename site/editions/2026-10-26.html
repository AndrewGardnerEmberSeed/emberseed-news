WA seeks Deloitte reimbursement over flawed merger report

The WA government is seeking expedited cost recovery from Deloitte after errors were found in a A$166,000 feasibility study on merging three state universities. Reported errors include AI-hallucinated references and a A$260m discrepancy in one cost scenario, according to The Guardian. Deloitte's report says it used generative AI for research collation and editorial proofing, and it earlier called the errors minor. The government says it has been advised the errors do not affect the modelling or findings, and Deloitte has not responded to Guardian questions.

Why it matters: If you hand research or reports to consultants, check contracts for AI disclosure, error checks and penalties, and ask how AI was used.

Source: The Guardian

Industry

Socceroo Volpato licenses AI clone to Callandor

Socceroo Cristian Volpato has licensed his AI likeness to California-based Callandor, which will seek brand deals using his digital 'synthetic twin'. Under the mid-September deal, Callandor gets exclusive rights to his likeness, voice, biometric profiles and performance data, and will help restrict content he hasn't approved. Callandor was founded in February and its campaigns are still in pilot. In early October, the AFL told betting partner Sportsbet to stop using AI images of players without their consent.

Why it matters: If you use a well-known face in your ads, get written consent that covers AI-generated versions. Player contracts with major Australian sporting leagues don't yet mention AI, so don't assume consent is covered.

Source: The Guardian

Gaming & entertainment

RoseBerry recuts Neighbours episodes into AI phone microdrama

Fremantle and RoseBerry Media are recutting old Neighbours episodes into a vertical drama for phones. The first microdrama centres on Margot Robbie's character, Donna Freedman, and runs to about 150 minutes. Fremantle says only original footage is used, and its RedSnapper editing tool creates no new scenes or synthetic voices. Several cast members say they weren't consulted, and the actors' union MEAA is assisting them.

Why it matters: If you hold old videos or recordings of staff or customers, check consent and usage rights before reusing them with AI.

Source: The Guardian

Industry

OpenAI wins customers from Anthropic with steep discounts

OpenAI is reportedly winning customers from rival Anthropic with steep discounts, according to The Information, as relayed by The Decoder. Rippling CEO Parker Conrad says OpenAI's share of his company's coding workload rose from 16 to 52 percent, while Anthropic's fell from 57 to 18 percent. Replit switched after OpenAI offered more than 30 percent off a contract worth over US$100 million a year. DataCamp's CEO says OpenAI's pricing cut his costs by 80 to 90 percent.

Why it matters: If your business pays for AI tools, ask your provider for current pricing before renewing. The reported deals involved large customers, so smaller teams may not get the same terms.

Source: The Decoder

Safety & policy

Pennsylvania law requires labels on AI-generated ads

Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro signed a law on Thursday requiring labels on AI-generated ads. Any ad, whether a still image, video, audio or text, that has been significantly modified or generated by AI must carry a label in the same format as the ad. The rule applies wherever an ad was made, so ads expected to air in Pennsylvania are covered. The Verge says the law has gaps: 'significantly modified' is subjective, and parody, satire and evidently fictional content are exempt. The wording also suggests only content creating a false consumer perception needs a label.

Why it matters: If you run ads that might air in Pennsylvania, check whether AI-edited visuals need labels. The Verge says the law's wording is vague, so what counts is unclear.

Source: The Verge

Trends

Apollo economist finds no AI profit lift outside tech

Torsten Slok, chief economist at Apollo Global Management, says AI has delivered zero financial payoff to large US companies outside the tech sector. In a recent note, he studied the S&P 493, the S&P 500 without seven tech giants including Alphabet, Microsoft and Nvidia, and found profit margins and revenue per worker appear flat. He argues that AI may be a great calculator, but business depends on judgment, accountability and trust, which the technology does not understand. Recent studies have also found that AI has yet to deliver significant productivity gains.

Why it matters: If you're weighing an AI spend, ask how it will lift sales or margins, and set a measure to check. Apollo's economist says big US companies outside tech aren't seeing that payoff yet, though that says little about small Australian businesses.

Source: TechCrunch · Also reported by CNBC Technology

Safety & policy

Fired OpenAI researchers dispute leak claims

OpenAI dismissed three safety researchers, Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni and Jasmine Wang, after an internal investigation into alleged mishandling of sensitive information. In interviews with the Wall Street Journal, the three deny sharing confidential information and say they were never told in writing what they supposedly leaked. OpenAI says the firings had nothing to do with the safety concerns they raised, though it says Signal, which they used, is not an approved tool. The researchers warn the case could discourage independent safety auditors from working with AI companies.

Why it matters: If you bring in outside specialists to audit your AI tools, agree in writing what confidential information they can and cannot share.

Source: The Decoder

Business

Authors accuse Patrick White Publishing over poor service, AI art

Authors allege Patrick White Publishing delivered poor service, charged hundreds for AI-generated illustrations and used another firm's Australian Business Number. Kerry Stewart says she paid $500, wants it back and says the company refused a refund before she removed a critical Facebook post. Tulin Salis says a quoted $400 package was followed by an email offering a $1899 ghostwriting bundle. Responding to one Trustpilot review, the company says it disputes several claims. It did not reply to the ABC's requests for comment. The literary estate of Patrick White has asked it to stop using the author's name.

Why it matters: If you hire a publisher or designer, get refund terms and total costs in writing, and ask whether any artwork is AI-made before you sign.

Source: ABC News
