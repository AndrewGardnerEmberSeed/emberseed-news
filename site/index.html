Safety & policy

OpenAI counter-sues Apple over alleged trade secret theft

OpenAI has countersued Apple in California, alleging Apple stole trade secrets through a former employee. The filing alleges Apple gained confidential information by rehiring a man who had spent six months at OpenAI. It also alleges Apple discourages hardware suppliers from working with OpenAI. Apple denies the counterclaims, calling them a transparent attempt to distract from its own case.

Why it matters: If staff mix work files with personal phones or cloud accounts, a departing employee can turn that into a dispute. Set clear rules for where company files live.

Source: Engadget · Also reported by BBC Technology, The Decoder

Business

AI booking bots frustrate UK restaurants as Meta launches Muse

Meta has launched its Muse AI assistant in the UK this week, encouraging users to let it shop and book restaurant tables for them. In the US, where Muse has been downloaded millions of times, the booking platform Resy has banned bots after agents refreshed pages thousands of times to grab reservations. Bouchon Racine co-owner Dave Strauss says an AI caller that sounded human phoned his restaurant, and some UK venues are returning to phone bookings. The Information Commissioner's Office says Meta committed to a voluntary monitoring scheme, and the Competition and Markets Authority says talks led to fairer terms.

Why it matters: If you take online bookings, check whether your platform blocks bots, since they can snap up all the reservations. Some UK restaurants are going back to the phone.

Source: The Guardian

Gaming & entertainment

Coders race to put leaked GTA 6 on PC

Coders and gamers are racing to run an unofficial PC version of Grand Theft Auto 6, days after its console launch. Rockstar has yet to announce a PC release, and a leaked Xbox build gives coders a starting point, according to the BBC. AI coding tools such as Claude Code are speeding up reverse engineering, and one tracking platform counts 40 active projects, per The Decoder. Take-Two has issued takedown notices against public projects on GitHub, and neither company has confirmed the leaked build is authentic.

Why it matters: If you sell software or digital products, AI tools can help outsiders rebuild your product in days, so review who can access your code and files.

Source: BBC Technology · Also reported by The Decoder, GamesIndustry.biz

Business

Amazon walls off AI shopping agents as sales stay niche

Amazon has walled off its site to AI shopping agents, and in September it blocked Meta's Muse, saying it never agreed to take part. Bain & Co. research puts agentic purchases at 0.4% of global e-commerce transactions. A Visa study found AI agents failed about 43% of shopping test runs, and who is responsible when an agent's purchase goes wrong remains unclear.

Why it matters: If you sell online, shoppers using AI agents may not find your site or may hit checkout barriers. Check your terms of service for who carries liability when an agent's order goes wrong.

Source: CNBC Technology

Gaming & entertainment

China's AI micro dramas leave human actors out of work

AI now makes most of China's new micro dramas, and human actors are losing work. ABC News reports that more than 90 per cent of China's new micro dramas in 2026 were generated by AI, including actors, voices and scenes. Government figures show about 1,600 new AI micro dramas launched daily in the first half of 2026. Oksana, a 26-year-old actor, says her bookings have dried up, yet she is hooked on the shows herself.

Why it matters: If your business buys video or acting work, expect suppliers to offer AI-made options. The report says small teams can now produce these short series on computers, so compare quotes carefully.

Source: ABC News

Safety & policy

UK committee seeks unredacted Anthropic safety report

UK Parliament's business committee wants Anthropic to release an unredacted version of an AI safety report. In a letter dated 27 November, committee chair Liam Byrne asked for the full METR report and the reasons for the redactions by 4 December. METR says Anthropic cut major parts of its 5 November report, including evidence it called most informative. The report flagged a substantial risk that Anthropic's AI development could speed up by a factor of 1.3 to 3.

Why it matters: If your business relies on AI vendors' safety claims, ask for the evidence behind them, not just summaries. Here, outside evaluators say most of the evidence was withheld.

Source: The Decoder

Safety & policy

Tighten Gemini privacy through its account and device settings

Engadget has published a guide to Gemini settings that limit what Google's AI can keep and see about you. Unticking two boxes in the Gemini History settings turns off content sharing for chat text, attachments, audio and Gemini Live videos. Pausing chat history cuts Google's retention from three months or more to about two months, but you lose the ability to resume old chats. The guide also covers disconnecting linked apps and revoking camera and microphone access.

Why it matters: If your team signs into Gemini with work accounts, your organisation can retrieve their chats. Check the settings and decide what staff should type in.

Source: Engadget

Industry

Nvidia's Huang returns, but AI stocks stay jittery

Nvidia shares rose more than 31% on Monday after Jensen Huang returned as chief executive. The gains faded quickly, with the stock slipping roughly 5.9% on Tuesday and 4% on Wednesday. CNBC's Investing Club says AI stocks kept struggling as US inflation hit 3.8% in October and new tariffs were announced. The Nasdaq Composite also posted its best session since April 2025 on Monday, rising 6.35%.

Why it matters: If your super or shares include tech stocks, expect sharp swings around AI news. Judge AI tools on what they save your team, not on share price moves.

Source: CNBC Technology
