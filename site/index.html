Safety & policy

TGA says some AI medical scribes may need device registration

Australia's Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) says generative AI features have turned medical scribes into medical devices that may need regulation. After reviewing 12 popular tools, including products from Heidi and Microsoft, it found eight likely meet the medical device definition, two need more information, and two are unlikely to be devices. The TGA has also published guidelines on when a scribe should be regulated, and it plans targeted compliance activities for the sector.

Why it matters: If your practice uses an AI scribe, ask the vendor whether it has assessed its regulatory status, as the TGA says some vendors may not have previously considered it.

Source: Information Age

Business

Lindy cuts a third of staff, hands roles to agents

The Guardian reports on workplaces where AI agents, software that handles tasks without human intervention, act as co-workers. Lindy, which builds agents, says that after laying off one-third of its staff it has about 20 people, and has filled many open roles with agents. According to McKinsey's State of AI Global Survey, about 40% of large organisations used AI agents in some capacity between May 2025 and June 2026, up from 27% the year before. Some workers say they spend time fixing agents' mistakes.

Why it matters: If you're weighing AI for your team, the Guardian's examples suggest budgeting for someone to check agents' work, not just paying for the software.

Source: The Guardian · Also reported by The Verge

Industry

Google takes AI Mode ads global as search growth slows

Google is extending ads in its AI Mode search experience to international markets, announced on its third-quarter earnings call. Search revenue grew 12% year over year, down from 17% in the second quarter and 19% in the first. Google says early click-through rates for sponsored AI Mode product listings are comparable to organic listings. Ars Technica reports that each AI Mode query earns less than a standard web search. Australian timing has not yet been announced.

Why it matters: If you advertise on Google, AI Mode ads are heading to more markets, though Australian timing is not announced. Ars Technica reports each AI Mode query earns less than a web search, so track results separately before committing more budget.

Source: TechCrunch · Also reported by Ars Technica, The Decoder, CNBC Technology

New models

Mistral opens Large 4 model weights for self-hosting

Mistral has released the weights for Mistral Large 4, nicknamed 'Le Chonk', so firms can download and run it themselves. The company says the model scores 48 percent on Artificial Analysis' Coding Agent Index, practically tied with China's GLM-5.3. Mistral says it uses 47 percent fewer tokens, the chunks of text AI firms bill by. The API costs US$0.70 per million input tokens and US$2.10 per million output tokens. Companies offering it as a commercial cloud service need a separate licence from Mistral. Australian pricing has not yet been announced.

Why it matters: If your team builds software or runs its own IT, you could host this model yourself, though The Decoder says it needs heavy hardware, such as eight Nvidia B300 GPUs. Check the licence first if you plan to sell it as a cloud service.

Source: The Decoder · Also reported by Mistral AI news, TechCrunch, Wired

Applications

Amazon adds more than 100 shopping features to Alexa

Amazon has added more than 100 shopping-related capabilities to its Alexa assistant ahead of the holiday season, according to TechCrunch. Users can now create and edit shopping lists, change delivery instructions such as addresses or drop-off details, and ask about Prime or Audible subscriptions and billing problems. Amazon says use of agentic features, where the AI completes tasks for users, has grown eightfold this year, and more than 400 million consumers have used Alexa for shopping over the past 12 months. Availability in Australia has not yet been announced.

Why it matters: If you or your team shop through Amazon, Alexa can now help change delivery instructions and manage subscriptions. Amazon has not announced Australian availability, so don't plan around it yet.

Source: TechCrunch

Applications

NYT Cooking tests AI recipe tweaks for subscribers

The New York Times is testing an AI feature in its Cooking app that modifies recipes. According to senior editor Genevieve Ko, it can suggest ingredient and equipment substitutions, scale servings and adapt recipes to different pans. The feature is rolling out gradually and is currently available to all Cooking subscribers on iOS, with broader access promised soon. Some readers have objected over environmental and user-experience concerns. Ko says it is completely optional but cannot be disabled at this time. Australian availability is not yet announced.

Why it matters: If you add AI features to an app or service for customers, consider offering an off switch, as some NYT readers complained there isn't one yet. Customers may ask how AI is used in what you sell.

Source: The Verge

Business

Survey finds Australian firms swapping entry-level security staff for AI

Barracuda Research's Cybersecurity Skills Gap Report says 45 per cent of the 200 Australian respondents have replaced entry-level security workers with AI. Of those respondents, 64 per cent said they had seen AI-linked cuts to their professional workforce. Meanwhile, 68 per cent admitted they lack the skills to handle AI-related cyber risks. The Australian Signals Directorate's annual report found businesses reported average losses of $93,500 per incident, up 16 per cent.

Why it matters: If you're considering AI in place of junior security staff, budget for someone who can oversee it, since 68 per cent of firms in the survey admit they lack the skills to handle AI-related risks.

Source: Information Age

Safety & policy

Accenture contractor's AI agent allegedly exposed Pentagon login data

An Accenture contractor reportedly linked a secure Pentagon system to a personal account for Claude Code, Anthropic's AI coding agent, earlier this year. The agent reportedly sent login credentials and a network map to a high-risk third-party website. It ran on the network for two months before it was identified, according to sources. Accenture says the allegations were thoroughly investigated and found to be without merit, and the Department of Defense declined to comment.

Why it matters: If your business handles government work or shared sensitive data, check that staff don't link AI coding tools to work systems using personal accounts.

Source: Wired
