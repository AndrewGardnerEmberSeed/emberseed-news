Safety & policy

WA suspends Deloitte for three months over AI errors

The WA government has suspended Deloitte from most government work for up to three months over an AI-riddled report. The report analysed a proposed merger of Curtin, Murdoch and the University of Western Australia. The government has ordered Deloitte to refund its A$166,000 fee, which Tertiary Education Minister Tony Buti says is still unpaid. Deloitte calls the errors "minor" and says they don't affect the findings.

Why it matters: If you hire consultants to write reports, ask how AI is used and who checks the output. Agree refund terms in writing before you sign.

Source: ABC News

Gaming & entertainment

Tidal bans fully AI-generated music from 9 December

Streaming service Tidal will remove fully AI-generated music from 9 December, deleting existing tracks as well. Music lead Robert Andersen says 43% of tracks uploaded in the past 90 days were AI-generated, and earlier labelling was not enough. Tidal estimates it will delete around 29 million songs and reject 80,000 to 85,000 tracks a day. Music made with AI assistance is still allowed, as long as AI isn't the sole output.

Why it matters: If you license music for your business or distribute tracks for clients, check any AI-made audio. From 9 December, Tidal will reject fully AI-generated songs, though AI-assisted work is still accepted.

Source: The Verge · Also reported by TechCrunch, Ars Technica, The Guardian

Business

Anthropic study finds AI adoption stalls on internal friction

Anthropic's "Working with AI" report finds that most roadblocks to workplace AI sit inside companies, not in the software. Researchers conducted 66 in-person interviews at 58 US companies, all Anthropic customers, so the sample isn't representative. Respondents say 55 percent of nearly 300 live AI projects raised output or quality, but 19 percent delivered no business value. The most common obstacle was the volume of AI output staff still need to check.

Why it matters: If you roll out AI in your business, budget for staff checking its output before you count the time savings. In this study, that checking was the top snag.

Source: The Decoder

Industry

Amazon reportedly in talks to let Meta's Muse shop

Amazon and Meta are reportedly in talks that could let Meta's Muse AI agent shop on Amazon's store. Muse users began hitting checkout errors on Amazon about two months ago. Fortune's sources say Amazon is concerned about advertising revenue, search quality and its relationship with customers. Any deal would need both companies' CEOs to approve it, and neither company has responded to requests for comment.

Why it matters: If you sell on Amazon, nothing is agreed yet, but a deal could change how AI shopping agents find and buy your products. Keep your listings accurate and up to date.

Source: The Verge · Also reported by TechCrunch

Applications

University of Sydney makes honours students defend theses orally

All students in the University of Sydney's one-year honours program must now sit an oral assessment, as AI makes written theses an unreliable measure. Formats differ by faculty: one student's pass-or-fail conversation counts for nothing towards his grade, while another's hour-long interview made up half of hers. The university says it can't reliably restrict AI use in honours theses and will take feedback into its 2027 planning. One student calls the demand insulting.

Why it matters: If you hire honours graduates, expect some to have defended their theses aloud, as Sydney, Melbourne, Monash and Australian Catholic University move this way. Ask candidates to walk you through their research.

Source: The Guardian

Industry

Amazon buys Decart to sharpen its Trainium chips

Amazon is acquiring AI startup Decart's technology and team in a deal reported at US$7.5 billion. The core asset is Decart Optimization Stack, software that helps AI models run better on non-Nvidia chips. Amazon says it has worked with Decart since 2025 to optimise its Trainium chips, and TechCrunch says the deal looks aimed at speeding that up. Amazon is not taking on Decart's world-model work, while Anthropic reportedly tried to buy the startup for a similar price.

Why it matters: If your business runs on AWS, this could eventually mean more options for Trainium-based computing. No timeline has been given, so there is nothing to act on yet.

Source: TechCrunch

Trends

Lenders grow pickier as Big Tech borrows for AI

Big Tech firms may find it harder to borrow for AI as bond investors become choosier. PIMCO, citing Goldman Sachs data, expects the biggest AI companies to sell about US$250 billion of bonds this year. Man Group analysis says Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Alphabet are on track to spend more on capital projects than they generate in cash in both 2026 and 2027. L&G's Matthew Rees says some BBB-rated names, including Oracle, trade more like single B debt.

Why it matters: If you pay for cloud or AI tools from big providers, their heavy spending and borrowing is worth watching. Check contract terms before committing to multi-year deals.

Source: CNBC Technology
